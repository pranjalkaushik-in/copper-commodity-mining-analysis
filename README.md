# ⛏️ Copper Commodity Price & Mining Equity Analysis

## 📌 Project Overview

This project analyzes the relationship between **copper commodity prices** and the performance of major mining companies using historical market data from **2018 to 2026**.

The analysis compares copper with three major mining equities:

- **Freeport-McMoRan (FCX)**
- **Southern Copper Corporation (SCCO)**
- **BHP**

The main objective is to understand how changes in copper prices are reflected in mining-company stock performance and volatility, while also identifying periods where company-specific factors cause stock prices to behave differently from copper.

---

## 🎯 Project Objectives

- Analyze the long-term performance of copper and mining equities.
- Compare cumulative percentage growth since 2018.
- Measure and compare daily volatility.
- Study the relationship between copper returns and mining-stock returns.
- Identify unusually large movements in mining stocks.
- Understand how mining equities can behave differently from the underlying commodity.
- Build an interactive Power BI dashboard to communicate the findings.

The volatility chart compares the daily return volatility of copper and the three mining equities.

The dashboard shows that **FCX has considerably higher volatility than copper**, with an approximately **1.98× volatility multiplier** for the selected period.

This highlights an important characteristic of mining equities: stock prices can experience larger movements than the underlying commodity because they are also affected by company performance, operating costs, production, market sentiment and broader equity-market conditions.

# 🔎 Key Insights

### Copper vs. Mining Equities

Copper experienced approximately **100% growth since 2018**, while SCCO showed substantially higher cumulative growth.

This indicates that mining-company returns can differ significantly from the performance of the underlying commodity.

### Higher Mining Equity Volatility

FCX shows considerably higher daily volatility than copper, with approximately **2× the volatility** in the analyzed period.

This demonstrates the additional market risk associated with mining equities compared with the underlying commodity.

### Commodity–Equity Relationship

The scatter plots show a generally positive relationship between copper returns and the returns of FCX, SCCO and BHP.

However, several observations lie away from the main cluster, showing that mining stocks can experience significant movements even when copper prices move relatively little.

### Market Events

The **March 2020 COVID-19 market crash** appears as a particularly volatile period across the dataset.

The dashboard also identifies individual large movements in mining stocks, demonstrating that company-specific events can have a major impact beyond commodity-price movements.

# ⛏️ Mining Industry Perspective

Copper is an important industrial commodity used in areas such as **electrical infrastructure, construction, renewable energy and manufacturing**.

For mining companies, copper prices can have a significant effect on potential revenue and profitability. However, equity performance is influenced by more than commodity prices alone.

Factors such as:

- Production levels
- Operating costs
- Mine performance
- Commodity demand
- Capital expenditure
- Company-specific events
- Market sentiment
- Broader economic conditions

can cause mining-company stocks to behave differently from copper prices.

This project therefore explores the relationship between **commodity exposure and mining-equity performance**.
